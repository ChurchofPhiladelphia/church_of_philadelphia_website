Epistle of Revelation

The Seven Covenants of Philadelphia

"These things saith he that is holy, he that is true, he that hath the key of David, he that openeth, and no man shutteth; and shutteth, and no man openeth..."

Revelation 3:7

The epistle begins with the confirmation of ultimate spiritual sovereignty. Christ presents himself in his absolute purity ("holy") and absolute reality ("true"). The message to Philadelphia is grounded in the absolute authority of the King, ensuring that the covenants that follow are irreversible and eternal.

"He that hath the key of David, he that openeth, and no man shutteth..."

Isaiah 22:22 / Revelation 3:7

The "Key of David" refers to royal administrative and spiritual stewardship. Drawn from Isaiah 22:22, it signifies the master key to the treasury and chambers of the Kingdom. In the context of Philadelphia, it represents the authority to delegate access to spiritual blessings and resources, unlocking doors that no earthly power can lock.

"Behold, I have set before thee an open door, and no man can shut it..."

Revelation 3:8

The open door represents divine opportunity, spiritual liberty, and unhindered pathways. It represents the church's outreach, private administration, and ministry capabilities which remain completely open. Even with "a little strength," the door remains open because it is sustained by the King's own key.

"...for thou hast a little strength, and hast kept my word, and hast not denied my name."

Revelation 3:8

Philadelphia is praised for its quiet faithfulness. Lacking massive material power ("a little strength"), it did not compromise the word of truth or deny the covenant name. In a world of spiritual dilution, the Church of Philadelphia holds fast to the scriptures and retains the purity of the message.

"Because thou hast kept the word of my patience, I also will keep thee from the hour of temptation, which shall come upon all the world..."

Revelation 3:10

The promise of sanctuary and safety. Because the church remains patiently focused on keeping the Word, it inherits the covenant shield of protection. It will be kept and preserved from the global testing ("hour of temptation") that tries those who dwell on the earth.

"Him that overcometh will I make a pillar in the temple of my God, and he shall go out no more..."

Revelation 3:12

The ultimate promise of architectural and spiritual permanence. A pillar is structural, supportive, and immovable. Unlike the historical city of Philadelphia, which suffered frequent, destructive earthquakes forcing citizens to flee, the overcomer becomes an immovable pillar in the heavenly sanctuary—established forever.

"...and I will write upon him the name of my God, and the name of the city of my God... and I will write upon him my new name."

Revelation 3:12

The writing of three sacred names represents ultimate ownership, citizenship, and identity. It seals the overcomer as belonging to the Creator, as an official citizen of the New Jerusalem, and as bearing the new character and authority of the King himself.

Physical Manifestation

Mission House Sanctuary

The Church of Philadelphia in fellowship with Mission House, the House of Brode and the House of Fisher are in the planning stages of our Mission House Campus in Scottsdale Arizona. The architectural concepts include a peaceful setting for private assembly of our members, a daily fellowship lounge and room for meetings and conferences to fulfil our Heavenly Father's Will on Earth.

Sanctuary Exterior

Constructed from split-faced Fossil Beige ledger stone panels blending naturally with the rugged Arizona desert peaks.

Worship Assembly Hall

Fellowship Lounge

A light-filled gathering foyer and coffee bar with warm timber ceiling textures designed for quiet fellowship.
